# PM (IP U2240) and standby: research notes for the twin

Gathered 2026-10-21 for the PIC32CM1216MC00048 (PIC32CM MC00 Curiosity Pro, EV15N46A); the
PIC32CM JH01 carries the same PM. Sources, most authoritative first: silicon (section 3), the data
sheet **DS60001638E** chapter 16 (PDF pages 133-139) and the clock chapters' RUNSTDBY/ONDEMAND
rules, the ATDF.

## 1. What the data sheet says

- Two sleep modes, entered by WFI (or SLEEPONEXIT): **IDLE** (SLEEPCFG.SLEEPMODE = 2) stops the CPU
  clock; GCLK0, MCLK and the clocks peripherals ask for keep running. **STANDBY** (4) stops the CPU
  and every clock nobody asks for: a peripheral asks for its GCLK channel when it runs in standby
  (its RUNSTDBY), and a clock source or generator then runs on demand (Table 16-2 and its notes).
- Wake-up: from IDLE any enabled interrupt; from STANDBY only asynchronous interrupts and those of
  peripherals running in standby (Table 16-1).
- STDBYCFG: VREGSMOD (regulator in standby) and BBIASHS (SRAM back-biasing, which stops the DMAC
  reaching SRAM in standby; also to be cleared when SysTick's interrupt is used across standby).
- SLEEPCFG resets to 0, a value the table calls reserved; STDBYCFG resets to 0x0400.
- Table 16-2's clock source rule: with ONDEMAND = 1 a source "stops if RUNSTDBY = 0". Silicon
  disagrees (section 3).

## 2. The probes

`firmware/hello/tests/sleepprobe.c` (S1-S7) and `sleepprobe2.c` (A1-A9): the RTC (ULP1K, 1024 Hz,
runs in standby) wakes the core 51 counts after the WFI; TCs count OSC48M / 48 / 16 = 62.5 kHz;
SysTick (24 bits) shows whether the CPU clock ran; a TC armed right before the WFI tests early
wake-ups. The debugger stays attached (DSU.STATUSB.DBGPRES reads 1).

## 3. Answers from silicon (2026-10-21)

1. **IDLE** stops nothing the probe could see: SysTick, every TC and the RTC run (A1, S1), and a TC
   without RUNSTDBY wakes the core (A6).
2. **STANDBY stops the CPU clock**: SysTick moved 640 cycles across a 50 ms sleep (A3-A9). The
   first standby after the debugger's reset ran SysTick for 24-30 ms of it (A2, S2), later ones
   not; TCs stopped normally in that first one. Likely debugger traffic; checks skip it.
3. **A GCLK channel runs in standby only while one of its peripherals asks for it** (an enabled
   peripheral with RUNSTDBY): TC0 without RUNSTDBY stopped (A2-A5) even with GENCTRL1.RUNSTDBY set
   (A5), while TC2 with RUNSTDBY kept counting (A2-A9).
4. **Everything on a running channel counts**: TC0 without RUNSTDBY counted through standby when
   TC1, on the same channel (25), had RUNSTDBY (S2, S3).
5. **A requested clock runs on demand whatever the source's or generator's RUNSTDBY**: TC2 and TC4
   with RUNSTDBY ran with OSC48M.RUNSTDBY = 0 and GENCTRLn.RUNSTDBY = 0 (A2, A9), against Table
   16-2's "stop if RUNSTDBY = 0". (With the debugger attached; not tried without it.)
6. **Wake-ups**: a TC without RUNSTDBY never woke the core from standby (A7, S3: it resumed
   counting after the RTC woke it); one with RUNSTDBY woke it on time (A8, A9).
7. **The RTC read after a standby is slow**: with COUNTSYNC the first COUNT read took 6-12 ms after
   the wake-up (the RTC delta read 57 counts for a 51-count sleep, and TC0 counted ~7 ms after the
   wake-up while the firmware waited).
8. Reset values: SLEEPCFG 0x00, STDBYCFG 0x0400 (S7).

Test firmware lessons: flush the console before a sleep (a pending UART interrupt wakes IDLE, and
the flush takes milliseconds); arm the wake-up timer after the flush, right before the WFI; give
each counter its own GCLK channel unless sharing is the point (TC0/TC1, TC2/TC3 share).

## 4. The twin

The CPU's WFI calls a sleep hook (a pp-qemu addition in target/arm: `ARMCPU.sleep_hook`, called
from `HELPER(wfi)` and when `arm_cpu_exec_halt()` leaves the halt). The SoC reads PM.SLEEPCFG
there; for STANDBY it tells GCLK which channels keep running (`mchp_gclk_set_standby()`: those
with an enabled RUNSTDBY instance, located by the generator from the ATDF as
`MchpInstanceInfo.standby_*`, and channels with no such instance) and MCLK to stop the CPU clock
(`mchp_mclk_set_standby()`; SysTick holds its count while its clock is 0, a fix in
`armv7m_systick.c`). The TC and TCC count up to the moment their clock stops and resume from there
(their clock-change hooks fold the ticks since the last event in first). The SoC's "standby" QOM
property does the same for tests. A reset leaves standby.

Not modelled: the debugger's effect on the first standby, the slow RTC read after a wake-up,
SLEEPONEXIT, STDBYCFG (regulator, SRAM back-biasing and the DMAC), a TSENS or ADC conversion
paused by standby (the models finish it), SERCOM in standby (the model has no baud timing to
stop), DSU.STATUSB.DBGPRES.

## 5. Open questions

- Whether a requested clock still runs on demand with OSC48M.RUNSTDBY = 0 when no debugger is
  attached (rule 5 was seen with one).
- The wake-up latency from standby.
- The DMAC and EVSYS in standby (CHCTRLA.RUNSTDBY, CHANNEL.RUNSTDBY).
